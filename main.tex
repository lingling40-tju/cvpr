\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement fine-tuning of vision-language navigation (VLN) policies commonly relies on destination success or similarity to a reference route. These signals leave an instruction's intermediate requirements unresolved: reaching the endpoint does not establish that the agent entered the requested room or passed the specified landmark. We study an online reward that represents an instruction as an ordered list of semantic events and verifies each event against observations before and after executed actions. A frozen vision-language model supplies a three-way verdict (complete, incomplete, uncertain); motion and stopping checks reject visually plausible but unexecuted events. Each event is rewarded at most once, and the process reward is bounded below the success bonus. We integrate EventTrace into multi-turn ActiveVLN/GRPO and compare it with destination-only training for 64 optimization steps across three matched seeds. All six checkpoints and the SFT initialization are evaluated on the same 1,839 R2R val-unseen episodes. EventTrace changes success rate by $+0.65$, $+0.44$, and $-2.88$ percentage points across seeds; its mean paired change is $-0.60$ points (sample standard deviation $1.98$). A single-AI blind review of 49 selected transitions, replayed with training-time image encoding, finds that 7 of 15 verifier completion predictions match its labels. Two subsequent independent human reviewers agree on only 33 of these 49 cases; their 16 disagreements remain unadjudicated. The method is operational, but these results do not establish a navigation improvement or reliable semantic verification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Vision-language navigation asks an embodied agent to follow a natural-language route through visual observations. The Room-to-Room (R2R) task introduced instruction-guided navigation in real indoor environments~\cite{anderson2018r2r}; VLN-CE moves this task into continuous control~\cite{krantz2020vlnce}. Success rate (SR) and success weighted by path length (SPL) capture destination attainment, yet a successful stop can coexist with skipped intermediate instructions. Instruction fidelity work made this gap explicit~\cite{jain2019cls}.

Recent reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) approaches give visual-language policies online or trajectory-level feedback. ActiveVLN performs multi-turn exploration with group-relative policy optimization (GRPO)~\cite{zhang2025activevln,shao2024deepseekmath}. VLN-R1 uses time-decayed action rewards~\cite{qi2025vlnr1}; Nav-R1 combines format, understanding, and navigation rewards~\cite{liu2025navr1}; SeeNav-Agent proposes step-level verifiable process rewards in another navigation benchmark~\cite{wang2025seenav}. Progress-Think learns semantic progress from visual histories and instruction prefixes~\cite{wang2026progressthink}; SACA audits stepwise landmark progress and routes mixed-success and all-failure groups differently~\cite{li2026saca}. Our question is narrower: can a navigation instruction's ordered, physical events provide a bounded reward during \emph{closed-loop} R2R-CE rollouts, without matching the policy's actions to a reference path?

We introduce \emph{EventTrace}, a modular reward on top of an existing multi-turn policy. The reward uses a short event list, a frozen visual verifier with an abstention state, deterministic checks of motion or stopping, and a once-only order-preserving tracker. The actor sees only the original egocentric observations and instruction; simulator displacement is available only to the reward. Figure~\ref{fig:example} illustrates the distinction between seeing a bathroom and actually entering it.

We evaluate the reward with matched three-seed training and complete R2R val-unseen coverage. The observed seed variation and an exploratory verifier audit limit the empirical claim: this implementation is reproducible but does not improve navigation consistently.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Path and instruction fidelity.} SR and SPL reward the destination and efficiency, whereas nDTW and coverage weighted by length score (CLS) compare trajectories with a reference route~\cite{ilharco2019ndtw,jain2019cls}. Reference trajectories can be useful supervision but do not directly answer whether a distinct visual event actually occurred when multiple valid routes exist. Our reward targets event completion during execution; route metrics remain complementary evaluation measures.

\paragraph{RL for VLN.} ActiveVLN is our implementation base because its environment can return feedback after each multi-action turn~\cite{zhang2025activevln}. VLN-R1 and Nav-R1 show other ways to shape VLN rewards~\cite{qi2025vlnr1,liu2025navr1}. Progress-Think aligns observation and instruction progress and co-trains a progress-aware policy~\cite{wang2026progressthink}; SACA combines a landmark auditor with scenario-dependent group optimization~\cite{li2026saca}. SeeNav-Agent emphasizes step-level verification~\cite{wang2025seenav}. EventTrace tests a narrower combination of a once-only ordered event state, an explicit uncertainty outcome, and motion gates against rewarding a landmark merely seen without the requested transition. Our pilot applies GRPO to \emph{trajectory-level} returns; it does not claim turn-level credit assignment or stronger navigation performance than these systems.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Task and event representation}
An instruction $x$ yields a list $E(x)=(e_1,\ldots,e_K)$, where $e_k$ contains a verb type, target phrase, and source span. Supported event types include \texttt{enter}, \texttt{pass}, \texttt{descend}, and \texttt{stop\_near}. A frozen vision-language model (VLM) proposes at most three events, restricted to source spans in the instruction. The eight-episode wiring pilot uses curated lists; the main 64-step training runs parse instructions automatically at episode start. Parser errors produce an empty list and no semantic reward. We have not independently audited parser accuracy.

In the curated pilot, each of the eight episodes contains a stopping event; there are also three \texttt{enter}, three \texttt{pass}, and three \texttt{descend} events, for 17 in total. These counts describe the pilot distribution, not the prevalence of event types in R2R. Table~\ref{tab:gates} shows the deterministic portion of the verifier. Each gate can reject completion before a VLM call, but passing a gate is never sufficient to award a point.

At turn $t$, the policy receives the instruction, action history, and current egocentric image $I_t$, and emits up to three low-level actions. The simulator executes these actions and returns $I_{t+1}$, pose change $\Delta p_t$, and task metrics. Only the current pending event $e_{k_t}$ is queried. The frozen verifier produces
\begin{equation}
v_t=V(e_{k_t}, I_t,I_{t+1},a_t,\Delta p_t)
\in\{\mathrm{Y},\mathrm{N},\mathrm{U}\},
\label{eq:verifier}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{U}$ denotes insufficient evidence. A view of a landmark is not by itself a completion verdict. For crossing and passing, a forward displacement check is required; descending requires a negative vertical displacement; stopping near a target requires an executed \texttt{STOP}. The VLM inspects whether the image pair supports the specified target and transition. These are deliberately conservative gates, not a learned proof of physical state.

\subsection{Ordered, bounded process reward}
Let $k_t\in\{1,\ldots,K+1\}$ index the first unfinished event. The event fires only when its verifier says $\mathrm{Y}$. Then
\begin{align}
c_t &= \mathbb{1}[k_t\leq K\land v_t=\mathrm{Y}],\\
k_{t+1} &= k_t+c_t,\qquad
r_t^{\mathrm{sem}}=\frac{\lambda}{K}c_t.
\label{eq:events}
\end{align}
A \texttt{N} or \texttt{U} verdict leaves $k_t$ unchanged. The tracker prevents repeated reward for the same landmark, enforces event order, and guarantees $\sum_t r_t^{\mathrm{sem}}\leq\lambda$.
Because only the pending event is checked, at most one event fires per turn even if the agent performs several actions. This avoids ambiguous credit when a model emits an action batch but can delay recognition of adjacent events. The tracker retains a confirmed event after later image changes, so a completed event cannot be unconfirmed by viewpoint drift.

The base destination term is the weighted-success reward already used by ActiveVLN, with nDTW weight zero. We add a fixed success floor $b$ to both arms:
\begin{equation}
R(\tau)=\sum_t r_t^{\mathrm{sem}}
+S(\tau)\left[b+\alpha\left(1-\min\left(\frac{d_T}{3},1\right)\right)\right],
\label{eq:return}
\end{equation}
where $S(\tau)$ is simulator success after \texttt{STOP}, $d_T$ is terminal distance in meters, $\alpha=15$, $b=2$, and $\lambda\in\{0,1\}$. Hence any success receives at least 2 while a failure receives at most 1 semantic point. This priority holds for this nonnegative reward configuration; it does not imply that the verifier is correct.
The bound also makes failure reward interpretable: partial event completion can distinguish two unsuccessful attempts without allowing a failed route to outrank a successful one solely through event bonuses. The \texttt{STOP} event is distinct from simulator success: it demands visual proximity to its instruction target, while $S(\tau)$ uses the simulator's goal region. Disagreement between these signals is precisely a case the proposed reward aims to expose.

For each training episode, we sample two trajectories and normalize their returns within the group for GRPO~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath}. Event rewards are emitted after each turn, but the current trainer sums them before constructing a trajectory advantage. Consequently, the implementation changes which trajectories are preferred, not which individual turn receives the policy gradient. Turn-level event timing is available in the log for future credit-assignment work.

\subsection{Verifier failure policy}
The verifier is a frozen Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model. A service timeout, malformed response, or uncertain decision yields zero event reward; it does not end the episode. The reward requires no reference action prefix. Simulator pose is used only for reward verification, never added to the actor observation. In the pilot, the fixed event manifest is matched to both episode ID and exact instruction text to avoid assigning another episode's event list; the main runs use online parsing.
The verifier sees two RGB frames resized to fit within $448\times448$ pixels, the event type and target, its source phrase, the executed action text, translation magnitude, and vertical displacement. Its output is a deterministic one-letter \texttt{Y/N/U} response; generated explanations are not treated as confidence scores. This makes the reward externally auditable through a saved per-turn verdict, although a forced-choice label is still fallible. The HTTP verifier is separated from the simulator so it can be frozen and logged independently of policy updates.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Environment and policy.} We use R2R train in VLN-CE, rendered from Matterport3D scans~\cite{chang2017matterport3d}, the ActiveVLN implementation, and an SFT-initialized Qwen2.5-VL-3B policy. Matterport3D data and derived imagery are subject to its \href{https://kaldir.vc.cit.tum.de/matterport/MP_TOS.pdf}{academic terms of use}. Each turn contains at most three grouped action commands. The configured limits are 12 turns and 36 commands; the strict budget check can permit one extra command, and a command may execute several simulator steps. Actor sampling uses temperature 1.2 and two independently sampled rollouts per item. GRPO trains on two A800 GPUs at learning rate $10^{-6}$ with batches of four episodes. The destination-only control sets $\lambda=0$; the event arm sets $\lambda=1$. Both arms have the same SFT initialization, destination reward, data order within seed, and optimizer budget.

\paragraph{Pilot and corrected main training.} We first replay eight training episodes with expert actions solely to inspect expected events, then run a two-step reward-wiring pilot with curated events. These replays are neither held-out labels nor training results for the main comparison. For the main study, each arm runs 64 optimization steps at seeds 11, 22, and 33: 256 train-episode exposures and 512 sampled rollouts per arm and seed. The event arm uses automatically parsed events. A preflight check detected that a fixed per-request sampling seed duplicated every two-rollout GRPO group and yielded zero actor gradients; we excluded that attempt, removed the per-request seed while retaining the engine seed, and reran all six models. Every corrected group has distinct sampled actions. The archive records the excluded attempt and corrected patch.

\paragraph{Full val-unseen evaluation.} SR is the fraction of trajectories satisfying the simulator goal condition; SPL also accounts for path efficiency. Mean terminal goal distance is in meters. Training rollout success and return are diagnostics, not held-out outcomes; returns differ in definition between arms. The SFT model and six corrected step-64 checkpoints run on the identical manifest of all 1,839 R2R val-unseen episodes from 11 scenes. We use one generation per episode, temperature 0.2, top-$p$ 0.8, a 12-turn limit, and at most 76,800 image pixels. Four Habitat shards per checkpoint divide the manifest without overlap. The evaluator uses the same prompt and environment-specific OpenAI-compatible request and image-resize shim for all seven models. The completion gate checks the exact episode-ID set and zero inference errors. Episode IDs are paired within each seed, but stochastic decoding uses only one generation per checkpoint and is an additional source of variation.

\paragraph{Blind verifier review.} Separately, we collect shortest-path follower trajectories from 16 different val-unseen episodes, of which 12 yield at least one automatically parsed event. Before querying the verifier, one AI-assisted reviewer inspects RGB image pairs, executed actions, and motion and fixes 49 three-way completion labels across those 12 episodes. For the reported replay, both frames use the training client's 448-pixel JPEG encoding. The sample includes apparent transitions, controls, and final stops. The original AI review was independent of verifier predictions but had no human ground truth. Subsequently, two independent humans labeled the same answer-free, randomized 49-item package without access to predictions or prior labels. Their files were locked before comparison. We report their agreement separately and do not treat unresolved labels as verifier ground truth. Selected class proportions are not representative of deployment traffic.

\paragraph{Reward reaches optimization.} In the first optimizer step, both arms sample the same eight action sequences and attain seven successes. For every matched trajectory, the difference in total return equals its semantic event score, including a failed episode 7110 trajectory that receives 0.5 for completing ``descend stairs'' but never reaches the destination. This verifies the wiring of Eq.~\ref{eq:return}; it is not evidence that event reward improves the policy.

\paragraph{What the verifier recognizes.} In diagnostic expert replays, four of eight episodes receive full event credit, two receive partial credit, and two receive none; the tracker confirms 11 of 17 curated events. \paragraph{Independent human review.} The two blinded human reviewers agree on 33/49 three-way labels (67.35\%; Cohen's $\kappa=0.459$). Agreement is 17/23 for \texttt{enter}, 6/10 for \texttt{pass}, and 10/16 for \texttt{stop\_near}; 16 cases remain disputed. The brief rationales include interpretations of motion \emph{toward} a landmark as completion and cases where a previously visited landmark cannot be established from the supplied before/after views. This shows that the local evidence and event rubric can be underdetermined; it does not isolate model error from label ambiguity. No model-versus-human precision or recall is computed before independent adjudication, and this deliberately selected pilot cannot estimate population accuracy.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The main risk is reward misspecification: the VLM may confuse doorways, and displacement gates may miss small valid movements. One-at-a-time verification may miss events completed together. Before/after frames limit timing to a turn; trajectory-level GRPO offers limited credit assignment to the decisive action.

The event tracker assumes a single valid execution order. We have not
independently checked whether the parsed sequence matches the instruction's
temporal constraints. The local verifier can miss completion that requires
history or occurs in an alternative order consistent with the instruction.
The frequency and navigation effect of these cases remain unmeasured.

The complete val-unseen comparison shows a negative mean effect and large seed variation, but 64 steps expose only 256 training-episode instances per run. For EventTrace, a larger training budget and repeated decoding have not been evaluated. The selected 49-example review now has two independent human labelers, but their disagreements have not been adjudicated; instruction parsing is also unaudited. We cannot isolate noisy verification, parser errors, reward scale, budget, or optimization as the cause. Separately sampled and adjudicated verifier and parser sets, followed by ablations of ordering, abstention, gates, reward budget, and turn-level advantage, are needed. This audit cannot establish event precision or recall.

\subsection{Positive trajectory weighting and completed replication}
\paragraph{Positive-only trajectory update.} After the inconsistent
terminal-RLOO replication, we froze a different on-policy update on
45 R2R-train fit scenes, with eight development and eight reserved scenes.
Both arms receive the same distance-weighted success-15 plus reference-route
nDTW-5 terminal score $R_i$. For four sampled trajectories, the candidate uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_i &= R_i + 15S_i, \\
A_i &= M_i\min\!\left\{1.5,\frac{[Q_i-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j\ne i}Q_j]_+}{5}\right\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $S_i$ is simulator success and $M_i$ is one only for successful
trajectories or failures with $R_i\ge2.5$. This prioritizes success and
removes negative trajectory credit, inspired by self-imitation
learning~\cite{oh2018sil}. The update uses fresh on-policy groups and a
peer-return baseline, without a replay buffer or learned value function.
Each weight is broadcast to action tokens, with observation tokens masked.
The matched GRPO control and candidate use the same SFT initialization,
512 fit rows, 64 steps, group size four, one policy epoch, sequence-mean
loss aggregation, and reference KL coefficient 0.01. Every step has a
nonzero actor gradient in both arms.
This comparison tests success prioritization, eligibility filtering,
fixed scaling and capping, and removal of negative trajectory credit
jointly. Contributions of individual components remain unmeasured.

On the frozen 256-episode, eight-scene development screen, candidate/control
successes are 51/17 (SR 19.92\%/6.64\%); paired SR and SPL both improve
by 13.28 percentage points. Both arms have exact coverage and zero inference
errors, and local independent recount agrees. A CPU navmesh reconstruction
matches every raw SPL: all successful paths are shorter than the initial
goal distance, so their SPL is one under the 3 m success region. Descriptive
scene-bootstrap intervals are $[8.71,19.35]$ points for both differences.
The first evaluation's wrong nDTW reference split was corrected before
this complete sweep; all partial outputs and failed logs were archived.
The original joint $+2$-point gate passed, and all six fresh matched
512-row, 128-step, group-four runs at configured seeds 11, 22, and 33
have now completed. Each arm records 1,024 episode exposures and 4,096
trajectories across two complete passes over the fixed 512 fit IDs.
All 128 stored actor gradients and KL metrics are finite and nonzero.
Training diagnostics are separate from the navigation evaluation below.

The reserved 256-episode screen has candidate/control successes
35/24, 23/23, and 30/27. Mean paired SR/SPL changes are $+1.82/+1.76$
points; descriptive configured-seed/scene intervals cross zero.
Every final checkpoint was then evaluated on all 1,839 val-unseen
episodes regardless of its reserved score. Coverage is exact and final
inference errors are zero. Table~\ref{tab:positive-full} shows a small
mean advantage over the updated GRPO control, $+1.11$ SR and $+1.07$ SPL
points, rather than the large development difference.

is unestablished; configured-seed summaries are exploratory descriptions.
The SFT reference is one decode, not three independent SFT training seeds.
The extra evaluation was frozen after the pilot and before scale outputs.
Reserved scenes may have been exposed in earlier SFT or exploratory work,
and val-unseen has been used adaptively. This follow-up neither establishes
clean unseen-scene benefit nor validates a deployable semantic verifier;
its reward uses privileged simulator success and a reference route.

\section{Conclusion}
We formulated a path-free semantic process reward for closed-loop VLN that verifies ordered instruction events against executed motion and before/after views. A wiring pilot confirms that the bounded reward enters online GRPO. In three matched 64-step training runs and complete 1,839-episode val-unseen evaluation, EventTrace has a mean paired SR change of $-0.60$ percentage points relative to destination-only training, with inconsistent seed effects. Two independent human reviewers agree on 33/49 selected transitions, but the remaining cases lack adjudication and the selected sample cannot establish verifier accuracy. The current method is reproducible; reliable semantic verification and a navigation benefit remain unproven.

\section{Scope of the supplementary evidence}
This material preserves the exploratory protocols, failed screens,
training audits, and post-result diagnostics summarized in the main text.
All reported navigation differences have episode-matched controls and
explicit coverage checks. The screens differ in episode sets, budgets,
and prior use; their absolute success counts cannot rank methods across
experiments. No unadjudicated semantic labels or offline reward rankings
are treated as navigation gains.

\paragraph{Budget conventions in the positive-trajectory follow-up.}
A post-hoc source and log audit finds that training budget exhaustion
unconditionally assigns task failure and zero outcome reward, including
nDTW; evaluation instead executes STOP at the turn limit before collecting
metrics. In the completed seed-11 control and candidate, respectively,
230 and 314 of 4,096 fit rollouts end strictly within $3\,\mathrm{m}$
but receive zero timeout reward. Both runs' 8,192 records and their original
budget identities pass independent compact recount. In the prior full
FP16 SFT evaluation, 238 of its 555 successes follow forced turn-limit
STOP, among 1,083 such terminations. These are distinct training and
evaluation trajectories; their counts are not counterfactual repaired SR.
The checked base source is also shared by the pending three-direction
pilot. This objective-convention difference motivates separately matched
time-limit-aware credit tests, without changing existing frozen runs,
metrics or gates. It does not identify a cause of degradation.

\section{Credit-estimator and group-size development}
\label{sec:credit-followups}
\paragraph{Turn-level credit assignment pilot.} We tested a
group-four, actor-only return-to-go/leave-one-out estimator inspired by
RLOO~\cite{ahmadian2024back} and REINFORCE++~\cite{hu2025reinforcepp}.
Each executed turn receives its remaining return relative to active
peer rollouts; observation tokens are masked. The candidate also adds
training-only simulator geodesic progress, so both credit assignment
and reward change. From the same SFT state and 256 train rows, it and
a destination-only GRPO control ran 64 steps; all candidate actor
gradients were nonzero. On a frozen 256-episode val-seen manifest
(53 scenes, exact coverage, zero inference errors), candidate/control
successes were 68/78: paired SR $-3.91$ and SPL $-3.72$ points.
The prespecified joint $+2$-point gate failed, so this mechanism was
not expanded. One seed and decode cannot isolate turn-level credit
assignment or establish unseen-scene gains. This is not a deployable
semantic reward.

\paragraph{Credit--reward factorial follow-up.} To separate the two
changes in that pilot, we completed a four-arm, group-four comparison
from the same SFT initialization and seed, with 256 training rows and
64 optimizer steps per arm. The arms cross trajectory-level GRPO versus
turn-level leave-one-out updates with terminal-only versus additional
training-time geodesic progress. On a new, disjoint 256-episode val-seen
screen (50 scenes; complete coverage and zero inference errors), the
outcome-only GRPO control succeeds on 97 episodes. The combined
turn/progress, terminal-only turn, and progress-augmented GRPO arms
succeed on 96, 98, and 106, respectively. Their paired SR/SPL changes
against control are $(-0.39,-0.14)$, $(+0.39,+0.68)$, and
$(+3.52,+1.51)$ percentage points. Although progress-augmented GRPO
shows a positive SR difference, its SPL difference misses the frozen
joint $+2$-point advancement gate. Descriptive scene-cluster 95\%
intervals for that arm span zero: $[-2.24,9.26]$ SR and $[-4.21,7.17]$
SPL points. An independent per-episode recount agrees with the paired
analysis after a documented null-ID schema repair to the verifier;
the reward, evaluator, and metric formulas were unchanged. These
one-seed development results do not establish unseen-scene benefit,
and the turn-level and trajectory-level rules also differ in effective
token update scale. The process signal uses privileged geodesic
distance, not the semantic event verifier.

\paragraph{Token-scale-matched terminal credit.} The factorial's
turn-level estimator divided each turn contrast by its action-token
count, whereas the trajectory GRPO control applied an order-one
advantage to each action token. We therefore tested a new group-four
terminal-outcome-only leave-one-out estimator that standardizes the
active-peer contrast and broadcasts it to each action token in the
turn. From the same SFT initialization, it ran 64 steps on 256 train
rows. On a third, disjoint 256-episode val-seen development screen
(38 scenes), candidate/control successes are 108/103; paired SR and
SPL are $+1.95$ and $+1.26$ percentage points. Each arm covers every
episode once with no inference errors, and an independent raw recount
agrees. Exploratory scene-cluster intervals span zero: $[-4.50,8.04]$
SR and $[-5.11,7.26]$ SPL points. Both improvements fall short of the
predeclared joint $+2$-point gate, so the original conditional
three-seed expansion was skipped. A subsequent user-directed, explicitly
post-result group-eight sensitivity and group-four three-seed expansion
were conducted separately. This one-seed screen does not establish
unseen-scene benefit or validate a semantic reward.

\paragraph{Post-result group-size sensitivity.} Keeping the 64-step,
256-row prompts and terminal-RLOO rule fixed, we sampled eight rather
than four trajectories per instruction. This doubles simulator rollout
work. The group-eight run had logged nonzero actor gradients on 63 of
64 steps; the remaining step had an all-failure group and zero
advantage. On the same 256-episode val-seen screen, the group-eight
checkpoint succeeds on 112 episodes, versus 103 for the destination-only
control and 108 for the group-four checkpoint. Its paired SR/SPL
differences are $+3.52/+2.82$ points versus control and $+1.56/+1.56$
points versus group four. Every arm covers all 256 episodes across 38
scenes, with no inference errors, and an independent raw-stat recount
agrees with the compact episode records. Descriptive scene-cluster
intervals for group eight versus control span zero: $[-4.92,12.13]$
SR and $[-5.28,11.10]$ SPL points. This comparison was requested after
the group-four advancement gate failed; the group-eight result does not
retroactively satisfy that gate. It is one-seed, adaptively selected
development evidence, with group size confounded by rollout compute.

\paragraph{Post-result group-four scale.} The separately authorized
expansion used the terminal-RLOO rule with group size four, 512 training
rows, and 128 optimizer steps for each of seeds 11, 22, and 33. Every
candidate and its same-seed destination-only control covered all 1,839
val-unseen episodes (11 scenes), with zero inference errors. Paired
candidate-minus-control SR/SPL differences are $+3.97/+3.30$,
$-3.26/-3.07$, and $+4.13/+3.00$ percentage points, respectively.
Their mean differences are $+1.61/+1.07$ points, with seed standard
deviations $4.22/3.60$ points. Exploratory seed-and-scene intervals
span zero: $[-3.03,5.12]$ for SR and $[-2.86,4.21]$ for SPL. The
compact per-episode recount agrees with the raw-stat verifier. The
opposite sign for seed 22 and the intervals preclude a robust-benefit
claim. Val-unseen had already influenced method development, so this
is larger development replication rather than an untouched test. It
does not establish that the group-eight pilot will generalize or scale.

\paragraph{Multimodal GAE actor--critic pilot.} We also tested
a PPO actor~\cite{schulman2017ppo} with masked token-level
GAE~\cite{schulman2016gae}, a separately initialized multimodal value
head, and terminal-outcome-only feedback. The group-four actor and critic
started from the same SFT model; 64 optimizer steps on 256 train rows
had finite value losses and nonzero actor and critic gradients at every
step. On an adaptively reused 778-episode val-seen development screen
(53 scenes), the GAE candidate succeeds on 22 episodes versus 272 for
the same-seed destination-only control: paired SR $-32.13$ and SPL
$-31.56$ percentage points. Both arms have exact episode coverage and
zero inference errors; two independent raw-stat recounts and a compact
export recount agree. Descriptive scene-cluster intervals are
$[-36.83,-27.43]$ SR and $[-36.19,-26.95]$ SPL points. Mean path
lengths are 2.79 versus 6.53 m, consistent with shorter candidate
trajectories but insufficient to identify why learning failed. The
prespecified joint $+2$-point scale gate failed, so the conditional
three-seed GAE expansion was skipped. This one-seed development result
is not evidence about unseen-scene generalization.
The frozen recurrence discounts each generated action-text token
($\gamma=0.99$, $\lambda=0.95$), skipping observation spans. A post-result
synthetic zero-value check shows text-length-dependent early credit at a
fixed decision count. Decision-clock GAE remains untested, and this
diagnostic does not identify the cause of the navigation decline.

\section{Representation-to-reward development}
\label{sec:representation-followups}
\paragraph{Instruction-grounded reward representation.} We also probe a
potential reward that combines a causal progress encoder from frozen
navigation-policy states with a SigLIP image--instruction encoder adapted
on training-scene goal panoramas and group-four successful/failed routes.
SigLIP text is padded to a fixed 64 tokens, and offline scoring uses
the same one-image request shape as the online service. Each component
is normalized by its fit-scene mean absolute margin; a fixed 1:1
mixture is applied without development-set tuning. On 52
development pairs, successful-route ranking rises from 37/52 for the
temporal term and 32/52 for the visual term to 43/52 for the mixture;
correct-versus-wrong-instruction preference is 39/52. On 38 new
episode-disjoint, train-scene pairs, the mixture scores 30/38 route ranks
and 34/38 instruction comparisons. A previously inspected 48-pair scene
audit scores 41/48 and 37/48. These selected offline pairs share train
scenes or prior audit use, so they provide a screen for an online
group-four reward comparison, not evidence of val-unseen navigation gain.

\paragraph{Online fused-reward screen.} We added this frozen potential as
a bounded terminal bonus to the destination reward and trained a matched
group-four policy for 64 steps at seed 11. Its training audit verifies
1,024 reward requests for 1,024 rollouts across 256 matched train episodes.
On the fixed 256-episode val-unseen screen, the fused-reward checkpoint
achieves 68/256 successes versus 80/256 for the same-data,
same-group-size destination-only control: paired SR changes by $-4.69$
percentage points and SPL by $-4.34$ points. Both arms cover exactly 256
episodes in 11 scenes with zero inference errors. This single-seed screen
does not support scaling this particular reward, despite its offline
ranking results. A failure-aware encoder trained on unsuccessful pairs
improves a separate 106-pair train-scene ranking audit from 69/106 to
79/106, but direct fusion with the image--instruction score weakens
reused instruction-matching probes. These findings do not establish
a navigation gain.

\paragraph{Failure-only reward screen.} We next applied the new temporal
potential only to unsuccessful rollouts, leaving successful rollouts on
the original destination reward. A matched seed-11 group-four policy
completed 64 steps on the same training episodes; its audit found 854
reward requests for 854 unsuccessful rollouts and no extra bonus for
170 successful rollouts. On the same fixed 256 val-unseen episodes, it
achieves 63/256 successes versus the destination-only control's 80/256:
paired SR changes by $-6.64$ points and SPL by $-6.05$ points. Both
models cover 11 scenes without inference errors. The exploratory
scene-cluster 95\% intervals are $[-11.07,-2.29]$ SR and
$[-10.66,-1.47]$ SPL points. This screen fails the predeclared positive
SR-and-SPL scale gate; we therefore do not run a three-seed scale-up for
this reward. Training-only failed-pair ranking (622/970 in all-failure
groups) does not transfer into a navigation benefit. A post-hoc behavior
check finds maximum-turn termination on 187 candidate episodes versus
107 control episodes, while mean traveled path length falls from 6.74
to 5.71 m; these differences motivate a stopping-aware reward test but
do not identify the cause of the regression. An offline equal mixture
with the adapted visual score also fails its train-scene development
gate (68/125 failed-pair ranks versus 90/125 for the temporal term), so
its audit remains unopened.

\paragraph{Stop-aware reward screen.} To test whether terminal-mode
conditioning mitigates this failure, we gave the frozen temporal bonus
only to unsuccessful voluntary STOP trajectories. Successes retained
the destination reward, while turn-limit and format failures received
no bonus. The matched seed-11 group-four policy completed 64 steps on
the same training episodes. Its audit found exactly 328 scorer requests
for 328 unsuccessful voluntary stops across 1,024 rollouts, with nonzero
actor gradients at every step. On a new, disjoint 256-episode val-unseen
screen covering 10 scenes, the candidate succeeds on 45/256 episodes
versus 93/256 for its same-seed destination-only control. Paired SR and
SPL changes are $-18.75$ and $-17.43$ percentage points; both arms have
zero inference errors. The predeclared positive SR-and-SPL gate failed,
so this reward received no three-seed expansion. Post-hoc analysis finds
mean path length 4.44 versus 6.93 m and only three candidate episodes
with a non-null termination reason versus 116 controls, consistent with
frequent early stops. On the candidate's train rollouts, its frozen raw
score has AUC 0.486 for distinguishing 18 unsuccessful STOP endpoints
within 3.5 m of the goal from 310 farther endpoints; only 11 of 183
positive-bonus stops are within 3.5 m. This train-scene diagnostic does
not establish causality, but it shows that failure-pair ranking is
insufficient calibration for rewarding STOP.

\paragraph{Mode-stratified ordinal screen.} To remove a direct reward
offset between unsuccessful STOP and turn-limit trajectories, we next
ranked frozen temporal scores only within the same terminal mode of each
four-rollout all-failure group; any-success groups retained the original
outcome reward. This is a trajectory-level group comparison, not true
turn-wise credit assignment: the current GRPO implementation broadcasts a
group-normalized total reward to all action tokens. The 64-step seed-11
training audit verified 1,024 rollouts, 149 all-failure groups with active
ordinal rewards, 827 scorer requests for exactly 827 failures, and a
nonzero actor update at every step. On a third fixed, nonoverlapping
256-episode val-unseen screen spanning nine scenes, the mode-stratified
candidate succeeds on 59/256 episodes versus 73/256 for its same-data,
same-seed group-four outcome-only control. Paired SR and SPL change by
$-5.47$ and $-5.23$ percentage points, with zero inference errors in
either arm. The exploratory nine-scene bootstrap 95\% intervals are
$[-9.06,-1.95]$ SR and $[-8.98,-1.74]$ SPL points. Among failures, the
candidate ends with an unforced model STOP 148 times versus 84 for the
control; this post-hoc association does not identify a causal mechanism.
The predeclared positive SR-and-SPL scale gate failed, so no 128-step or
three-seed extension was run for this reward. Absolute success rates
across the three 256-episode screens are not directly comparable because
their episode sets differ.

\paragraph{Privileged turn-wise credit upper bound.} The preceding
trajectory-level rewards broadcast one return to every action token. To
isolate whether finer credit assignment can help, we tested a
\emph{training-only} oracle that labels each executed turn by its change
in simulator geodesic distance. In all-failure groups of four, it
centers return-to-go among active rollouts at each turn and gives no
auxiliary advantage to generated STOP tokens; groups containing a
success retain destination-outcome GRPO. Geodesic distance is never a
policy observation, but this oracle is not a learned semantic reward.
The same-data seed-11 pilot completed 64 steps. An independent training
audit checked 256 four-rollout groups, 11,450 turns, active contrast in
all 141 all-failure groups, and nonzero actor gradients on all 64
steps. On a fourth, pre-frozen 256-episode val-unseen screen spanning
nine scenes, it succeeds on 81 episodes versus 72 for its outcome-only
control: paired SR $+3.52$ and SPL $+3.76$ percentage points, with
zero inference errors. The exploratory scene bootstrap 95\% intervals
span $[-2.73,9.13]$ SR and $[-2.58,9.28]$ SPL points. This one-seed
screen selected a same-checkpoint full val-unseen recheck. Across all
1,839 episodes, the oracle succeeds on 602 versus 545 for the control:
paired SR $+3.10$ and SPL $+3.05$ points. On the 1,583 episodes outside
the reused screen, it succeeds on 521 versus 481: SR $+2.53$ and SPL
$+2.50$ points. Both evaluations have exact coverage and zero inference
errors. This remains a post-screen, one-seed development result using
privileged training labels; it neither validates EventTrace's semantic
verifier nor establishes a deployable learned reward.

We next scaled this privileged mechanism to 512 distinct training episodes
and 128 optimizer steps at each of three seeds, with four rollouts per group
in both oracle and destination-only arms. All six checkpoints were decoded
once on the same 1,839 val-unseen episodes, with exact coverage and zero
inference errors. Table~\ref{tab:turnwise-oracle-scale} gives paired
results. The mean oracle change is $+2.47\pm3.18$ SR and
$+2.51\pm2.78$ SPL points (sample SD over seeds); seed 22 instead
loses $1.09$ SR points. On the 1,583 episodes outside the reused
256-item development screen, the means are $+2.63$ SR and $+2.64$
SPL points. Exploratory scene-and-seed intervals for the full-set
mean are $[-1.49,6.05]$ SR and $[-1.20,5.78]$ SPL points. Thus the
observed mean is positive, but the seed variation and intervals do not
support a consistent gain. The reward uses simulator distance.

An adaptive stopping-boundary pilot tested whether capping geodesic
progress credit inside the 3 m success region and penalizing further
movement there by 0.1 improves this oracle. Its seed-11 matched n=4
64-step audit verified 256 identical training rows, nonzero actor
gradients at every step, and 902 inside-boundary penalty turns. On the
reused 256-episode development screen, the candidate succeeds 67 times
versus 72 for the unchanged control: paired SR $-1.95$ and SPL $-1.62$
points, with zero inference errors. This fails the fixed positive
SR-and-SPL scale gate. Neither privileged variant establishes a
deployable semantic reward.

Two observation-only progress representations were screened before
online use. An instruction-conditioned scalar potential fit to cached
policy RGB histories reached only 48.88\% balanced forward/regression
accuracy on its small train-scene development split. A joint two-view
antisymmetric LoRA reached 53.52\% balanced accuracy and 52.08\%
correct-versus-wrong-instruction preference at its selected checkpoint;
at 9.88\% stationary false positives, its forward recall was zero.
Neither passed the fixed development gate or entered RL. A further
start-anchored, action-conditioned three-view LoRA also failed its
small development gate (55.51\% balanced direction, 22.22\% regression
accuracy), so it received no audit or online reward test. This leaves
distillation of the privileged credit signal into an observation-only
representation as open work.

We also screened arrival evidence on a scene-disjoint R2R-train split
of audited group-four policy rollouts. A single-frame occupancy head
reached AUC 0.6053, but recalled only 8.33\% of inside-region views
and 5.04\% of inside-region views on unsuccessful routes at a pooled
false-positive rate below 5\%. A separate joint two-view, instruction-conditioned
transition head was trained for a fixed 1,024 microsteps on real
crossings, far nonarrivals, real near-goal retreats, and identical-view
wrong-instruction pairs. On all 758 development pairs from 351 records,
its selected checkpoint reached AUC 0.6430, versus 0.5177 for a
text-only control and 0.5211 after same-scene image-pair shuffling.
For identical image pairs with different instructions, the correct
instruction ranked higher in only 98/176 cases (55.68\%). A CPU-only
source audit confirms that these pairs share the exact simulator states;
their wrong-instruction labels are geometric alternate-goal proxies,
without independent semantic adjudication. Weak grounding and label
ambiguity cannot be separated by this diagnostic.
Nevertheless, at 4.89\% pooled false positives it recalled only
15.63\% of crossings and 13.45\% of unsuccessful crossings, below the
frozen 55\% and 50\% gates. These exploratory screens reused
development scenes across method design; neither opened its reserved
train-scene audit or entered online RL. Better AUC therefore supplies
no evidence of a navigation gain from this learned reward.
